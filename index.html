Wearable IoT · Edge AI · Fall Detection · Biomedical Engineering

LifeTrack: A Low-Cost Edge-First Wearable IoT Platform with Measured Cryptographic Overhead and Edge-Resilience for Real-Time Health Monitoring and Fall Detection

LifeTrack is a low-cost wearable health-monitoring prototype built around a dual-core ESP32. It combines physiological sensing, motion-based fall detection, local edge processing, and GSM emergency communication. The design aims to remain useful when Wi-Fi or internet connectivity is unavailable.

$45Reported bill of materials
5,000Reported adult volunteers in physiological validation

System architecture

  • Dual-core ESP32 with FreeRTOS task separation.
  • Sensor-processing tasks separated from web-server and GSM communication tasks.
  • Local web dashboard hosted on the device for offline access.
  • GSM-based SMS/voice emergency alerting independent of Wi-Fi.
  • AES-256-CBC encryption for data before optional cloud transmission.

Hardware components

  • MAX30100 — heart rate and SpO₂ sensing.
  • MPU6050 — acceleration and angular-motion sensing.
  • MLX90614 — non-contact temperature sensing.
  • DS3231 — real-time clock.
  • NEO-6M — GPS positioning.
  • SIM800L — GSM communication and emergency alerts.

Fall detection and machine learning

The embedded fall-detection logic uses a temporal sequence of freefall, impact, and post-impact immobility. In the reported simulated-event evaluation, sensitivity was 96.24% and specificity was 98.05%. Precision was 57.40%, corresponding to an estimated 2–4 false alarms per 24 hours in the reported test conditions.

On the public SisFall dataset, the study reports Leave-One-Subject-Out (LOSO) evaluation. XGBoost achieved the strongest reported balanced accuracy (84.5%) and sensitivity (81.03%) among the compared models. CNN-1D and BiLSTM did not outperform XGBoost. An ablation analysis indicated that temporal context is important and single-snapshot classifiers are inadequate for this task.

Security and edge-resilience

  • AES-256-CBC encryption was evaluated for its computational and power cost; the reported additional power was approximately 18 mW.
  • With Wi-Fi disabled, reported GSM fall-alert time remained similar: approximately 9.4 seconds versus 9.1 seconds in the comparison condition.
Research contribution: LifeTrack investigates the feasibility of combining low-cost wearable sensing, local processing, security-cost measurement, and connectivity-independent emergency communication in one embedded platform.

Limitations and responsible interpretation

  • The prototype is intended for non-clinical trend monitoring and is not a diagnostic or treatment device.
  • Fall events were simulated by younger adults on a padded mat; performance in real-world older-adult falls remains unestablished.
  • The reported prototype weighs approximately 180 g and uses a 2G SIM800L module, whose network availability varies by country and operator.
  • Security limitations include firmware-embedded keys, an HTTP local dashboard, and SMS command authentication based on phone number. The system should not be considered secure against all adversarial attacks.
  • Participant sex, BMI, and skin tone were not recorded, limiting subgroup analysis of optical-sensor performance.
  • Stress, sleep-efficiency, and calorie estimates were not validated against medical reference standards.
  • The reported false-alarm rate requires further reduction and prospective real-world evaluation before safety-critical use.
Cyber-Physical IoT · Remote Patient Monitoring

MediEdge: A Low-Cost Edge-Based Cyber-Physical IoT Platform for Real-Time Remote Patient Monitoring with Comparative Validation

MediEdge is a low-cost remote patient-monitoring platform developed using an ESP32-based embedded architecture and physiological sensors. The system explores local acquisition and processing of vital-sign data with secure cloud synchronization.

  • AD8232 ECG sensor and waveform processing.
  • MAX30100 for heart rate and SpO₂.
  • DS18B20 body-temperature sensing and DHT11 ambient monitoring.
  • Encrypted cloud synchronization using AES-256-CBC and HMAC-SHA256, with timestamps and replay checks.
  • ECG processing includes baseline correction, 50 Hz notch filtering, low-pass filtering, and QRS analysis.

Reported platform indicators: approximately $34.50 bill of materials, 4.3-hour battery operation, 98.7% synchronization success, and 8.2 ± 2.1 seconds reconnection time.

Publication: Published in Measurement (Elsevier), Vol. 292, 2026.
